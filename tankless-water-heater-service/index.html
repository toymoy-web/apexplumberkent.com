Overview

Keeping A Tankless Unit Running Right

Tankless water heaters heat water on demand rather than storing it, which means no standby tank to develop the sediment problems a traditional water heater eventually has. But the heat exchanger inside a tankless unit still needs regular maintenance, specifically an annual flush with a descaling solution to clear mineral buildup, or performance drops off in a way that mimics a bigger problem.

Most manufacturers, including the major residential brands installed in this area, specify an annual flush as a condition of keeping the warranty valid. It's a service that's easy to skip because a tankless unit doesn't fail all at once the way a tank leaking on the floor does. Instead it just gradually delivers less hot water for the same demand, until eventually the difference is hard to ignore.

Kent Context

Tankless Units In Kent's Newer Neighborhoods

Tankless systems show up most in newer East Hill and Panther Lake developments and in recent townhome communities near downtown and along the West Hill Interstate 5 corridor. Annual flushing matters for any tankless unit, so we schedule it as part of the service instead of leaving it to chance.

Signs You Need This

Signs Your Tankless Unit Needs Service

Water Runs Lukewarm, Not Hot

A gradual drop in max temperature over months, rather than a sudden loss of hot water, usually points to scale buildup rather than a failed part.

Unit Makes Popping Or Rattling Noises

Mineral deposits on the heat exchanger can cause noise during operation that wasn't there when the unit was new.

Error Code On The Display

Most tankless units show a specific error code when something's wrong. We can diagnose most codes from major brands over the phone before a visit.

It's Been Over A Year Since The Last Flush

If you can't remember the last descale service, that's reason enough to have it checked before a real problem develops.

What This Usually Means

Lukewarm Water From A Tankless Unit Is Usually Scale, Not A Failed Heater

If you've searched for a plumber because your tankless water heater seems to be losing power, the instinct is to assume the unit itself is dying. In the large majority of cases, especially on a unit that's a few years old with no service history, the real cause is mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, restricting how efficiently it can transfer heat. A flush and descale, not a replacement, is usually the actual fix.

How We Fix It

Flush, Descale, And Repair

A standard flush service circulates a descaling solution through the unit's heat exchanger for roughly 45 minutes, clearing mineral buildup and restoring the unit's ability to transfer heat efficiently. We check the inlet filter and inspect for any error history at the same time, since those small maintenance items are often what actually resolves an intermittent complaint.

If the unit has a genuine component failure rather than a maintenance issue (a failed igniter, a bad flow sensor, or a cracked heat exchanger), we'll diagnose it honestly. You'll get a straight comparison between repairing the specific part versus replacing the unit, based on its age and the actual cost of the fix.

FAQs

Common Questions

How often should a tankless water heater be flushed?

Most manufacturers recommend an annual flush, and skipping it is one of the most common reasons we see performance complaints on units that are otherwise in good shape.

Can you fix my tankless unit or do I need a new one?

It depends on what's actually wrong. We diagnose the specific issue first and give you an honest comparison between repair and replacement, rather than defaulting to either one.

Why is my water not as hot as it used to be?

In a tankless unit, this is most often mineral scale on the heat exchanger, especially if it's been over a year since the last descale service.

Do you work on all tankless brands?

We service the major residential tankless brands installed in this area. If you're not sure which brand you have, we can usually identify it from a photo of the unit's data plate.
