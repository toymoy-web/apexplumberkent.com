Overview

Drain Clearing for Kent Homes

Most drain calls in Kent fall into two groups: a single fixture that's slow or clogged, and a whole-house backup that points to the main line. We treat these differently from the first visit, because snaking a kitchen sink and clearing a main sewer line under the yard are not the same job, even though both start with the same complaint of water not going down.

Kent's housing stock plays a real part here. Homes on the valley floor, built in the 1950s and 60s, often still run original clay or cast iron sewer laterals. Those materials develop cracks and joint separations over decades, and the mature street trees common in those older neighborhoods send roots straight through the gaps looking for moisture. A snake can clear the immediate clog, but if roots are the actual cause, the same spot clogs again within months unless the line itself gets addressed.

Signs You Need This

Signs You Have A Real Clog, Not Just A Slow Day

Slow Drain That's Getting Worse

A sink or tub that used to drain fine and now takes longer each week, rather than a one-time slow flush.

Gurgling From Other Fixtures

Running one fixture makes another gurgle or bubble, which usually means trapped air from a partial blockage further down the line.

Sewer Odor Near A Drain

A persistent smell near a floor drain or the lowest fixture in the house often means the trap has run dry or the main line is partially blocked.

The Same Clog Keeps Coming Back

If snaking clears it but it returns within a few weeks, that's a signal the real cause (roots, a break, or grease buildup) hasn't actually been fixed yet.

How We Fix It

How We Clear It And Confirm The Cause

For a single slow fixture, a cable auger run directly through the drain usually clears the blockage in one visit. For a main line backup, or a clog that keeps returning, we run a sewer camera down the line first. That's the only real way to tell the difference between a soft grease clog, a root intrusion, and an actual crack or offset joint in the pipe, and it means we're not guessing at the fix.

When roots or grease buildup are the real cause, hydro jetting clears the line far more completely than a cable, because it scours the full width of the pipe with high-pressure water rather than punching a hole through the clog. If the camera shows a genuine structural problem, like a collapsed or offset section of clay pipe, that's a repair job, not a cleaning job, and we'll tell you honestly which one you're actually looking at before any work starts. Any pipe repair or replacement work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which Washington has adopted statewide.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do you offer camera inspection?

Yes. Any clog that keeps coming back, or a full main line backup, gets a camera run before we recommend a fix, so you're not paying to snake the same root intrusion twice.

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

For most clay, cast iron, and PVC lines in good structural condition, yes. If the camera shows a pipe that's already cracked or badly corroded, we'll recommend cabling or repair instead, since high pressure isn't the right tool for a pipe that's already failing.

Why does my kitchen drain keep clogging?

In older Kent homes, it's usually a combination of grease buildup in the line and a belly or low spot in the pipe run where waste collects. A camera inspection tells us which one it actually is.

Can you clear a main sewer line the same day?

In most cases, yes. If the camera shows a structural repair is needed rather than a cleaning, that becomes a separate scheduled job, and we'll walk you through the options before booking it.
