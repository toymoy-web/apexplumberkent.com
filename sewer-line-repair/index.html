Overview

Repairing A Damaged Sewer Line

A sewer line repair addresses actual physical damage to the pipe itself: a cracked joint, a collapsed section, or root intrusion severe enough that jetting alone won't hold. We confirm the damage with a camera first, then recommend the least invasive repair method that will actually last.

In Kent's older neighborhoods, clay and cast iron laterals from the 1950s and 60s are the most common candidates for this kind of repair, especially where mature street trees sit near the line's path.

Kent Context

Lateral Problems In Kent's Older Neighborhoods

Sewer lateral failures in Kent cluster where the pipe is oldest, on the valley floor and the older streets around downtown, and where mature trees have had decades to find a joint. Trenchless lining can often avoid tearing up a yard, and the camera tells us whether it is an option.

Signs You Need This

Signs You Need A Sewer Line Repair

Sewage Backup Into The House

Waste backing up into a tub, shower, or floor drain is often a sign of a significant blockage or line break.

Sinkhole Or Soggy Spot In The Yard

A depression or unusually wet area along the line's path can mean the pipe has collapsed underground.

Camera Inspection Confirms Damage

A crack, offset joint, or collapse seen on camera is a repair, not a cleaning job.

Sewer Odor With No Clear Source

A persistent sewer smell outside, near the line's path, can indicate an underground break.

How We Fix It

Trenchless Repair When The Line Allows It

For many line breaks, trenchless pipe lining can repair the damaged section from the inside, without digging up the yard. A liner is inserted through the existing line and cured in place, creating a new pipe wall inside the old one.

Not every line is a candidate for trenchless repair. A fully collapsed section or severe misalignment sometimes still requires traditional excavation. We'll confirm which approach fits your specific situation from the camera footage before recommending either one.

For a line with a history of root intrusion, a root barrier or periodic maintenance jetting can meaningfully extend the time between repairs, especially where a mature tree isn't going anywhere. We'll discuss whether that's worth adding once the immediate repair is fully handled and confirmed working.

Emergency Response

Emergency Sewer Backup And Line Repair In Kent

Sewage backing up into a tub, shower, or floor drain, or several fixtures gurgling at once, is a sewer emergency. Stop using water, keep people and pets out of the affected area, and call our 24/7 emergency line.

We confirm the cause with a camera, restore the line to service, and explain permanent repair options, including trenchless methods where the pipe allows. An emergency call does not mean an unexplained estimate.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is emergency sewer line service available in Kent?

Yes. We answer sewer backups 24/7 across Kent and the surrounding service area. Stop using water and call (253) 275-5116, and we will diagnose the line with a camera.

Do you have to dig up my yard?

Not always. Trenchless lining repairs many damaged sections from the inside. We'll confirm if your line qualifies once we've seen the camera footage.

How do I know if it's a repair or just a clog?

A camera inspection tells us for certain. A clog clears with cabling or jetting. Actual pipe damage needs a physical repair.

Is a sewer backup an emergency?

Yes, and we treat it that way. An active backup into the home needs a same-day response.

How long does trenchless repair take?

Most trenchless repairs are completed in a single day, though curing time for the liner can extend the full process slightly.

Will roots come back after a repair?

A proper repair removes the damaged section entirely, but a nearby tree can still send new roots toward the line over time, which is why we sometimes recommend a root barrier on a repeat-offender line.

How is the repair area restored afterward?

For excavation repairs, we backfill and restore the disturbed area as close to its original condition as reasonably possible once the pipe work is complete.
