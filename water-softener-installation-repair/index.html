Overview

Do You Actually Need A Softener?

A water softener removes the calcium and magnesium minerals that cause hard water buildup on fixtures, pipes, and appliances over time. King County's municipal supply is generally soft compared to much of the country, which means the case for a softener in Kent is genuinely weaker than in hard-water regions, and we'll say so rather than selling one you don't need.

That said, some Kent homes on private wells, or homes with older galvanized plumbing that's already accumulated mineral scale buildup, do see real, measurable benefit from a softener. We assess your actual water and plumbing before recommending one.

Signs You Need This

Signs Worth Checking

Scale Buildup On Fixtures

White, chalky buildup around faucets or showerheads can indicate harder water than the municipal average, especially on a private well.

Soap Doesn't Lather Well

Poor lathering can be a hard water sign, though it's less common with Kent's typical municipal supply.

On A Private Well

Well water hardness varies significantly by location and isn't covered by the municipal water reports at all.

Existing Softener Not Working Right

A softener that's stopped regenerating properly, or is using excessive salt, likely needs a repair.

How We Fix It

Testing, Installation, And Repair

For a new softener request, we test your actual water hardness rather than assuming based on general averages, since private wells and older plumbing can both change the picture. If a softener genuinely makes sense, we size and install a system matched to your household's water use.

For an existing unit, we diagnose whether the issue is the resin bed, the control valve, or the brine tank, and repair the specific component rather than defaulting to a full system replacement.

Salt-based systems genuinely remain the most effective option for real hard water, though salt-free conditioning systems exist for households that want to avoid added sodium in their water. Salt-free systems condition minerals rather than fully removing them, which works differently and isn't the right fit for every situation. We'll walk through the trade-offs based on what your water test actually shows.

FAQs

Common Questions

Does Kent have hard water?

The municipal supply here is generally soft compared to much of the country. A private well can be a different story, which is why we test rather than assume.

Will you tell me if I don't need one?

Yes. We're not going to recommend a softener your water doesn't call for.

My softener uses too much salt. Is that a repair?

Usually, yes. That's often a sign the control valve or timer needs adjustment or repair rather than the unit needing full replacement.

How long does installation take?

A standard installation is typically completed in a few hours.

Does a softener waste a lot of water?

A properly sized and programmed system uses a modest amount of water during its regeneration cycle. An oversized or poorly maintained unit can waste more than necessary, which is something we check during any repair visit.

Can a softener be added to an existing home?

Yes, most homes can have a softener added at the main water line without major plumbing changes, and we'll confirm the best location during an on-site assessment.

How long does a typical softener system last?

A well-maintained system commonly lasts 10 to 15 years before the resin bed or control valve needs major attention.
