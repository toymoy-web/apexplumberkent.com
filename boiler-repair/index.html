Overview

Diagnosing Boiler Problems

Residential boiler systems are less common than forced-air furnaces in newer Kent construction, but they're still found in a real number of older homes, especially on the valley floor, where radiant or radiator heat was original equipment. When a boiler starts acting up, the cause is usually one of a few specific issues: lost pressure, a failed circulator pump, a faulty thermostat, or mineral buildup in the system.

We diagnose the specific failure before recommending a fix, since a boiler repair is usually a fraction of the cost of a full system replacement, and most issues don't require one.

Kent Context

Boilers In Kent's Older Buildings

Boilers are uncommon in Kent's newer hillside subdivisions, which are mostly forced air, so most boiler calls come from older homes and small commercial buildings. If you have just bought a home with radiators, a service check before your first winter is worth the visit.

Signs You Need This

Signs Your Boiler Needs Repair

No Heat Or Uneven Heat

Cold radiators in some rooms but not others often points to air in the system or a circulation issue.

Strange Noises

Banging or gurgling sounds usually mean air trapped in the lines or mineral buildup in the system.

Pressure Gauge Reading Low

A boiler that keeps losing pressure has a leak somewhere in the system that needs to be located.

Leaking Around The Unit

Any visible moisture around the boiler itself should be checked before it leads to a bigger failure.

Safety First

Before We Arrive

Know Your System's Shutoff

Locate the boiler's water and, if applicable, gas shutoff valves in case the issue worsens before we arrive.

Leave A Gas Smell To The Professionals

If you smell gas near a gas-fired boiler, leave the area and contact your gas utility's emergency line before calling us.

Don't Bleed Radiators Under Pressure

If you're unsure how to safely bleed a radiator, it's better to wait for a technician than risk a burn from pressurized hot water.

Note Any Pressure Gauge Readings

If you've checked the pressure gauge, jot down the reading. It helps us diagnose faster on arrival.

How We Fix It

How We Repair A Boiler

We check system pressure, inspect the circulator pump, and test the thermostat and control components to isolate the actual failure. Bleeding trapped air from radiators resolves a surprising number of uneven-heat complaints on its own, often without needing any parts replaced at all.

For a leak, we trace it to its source, whether that's a fitting, the expansion tank, or the boiler itself, before recommending a repair. If the unit is old enough that repair costs approach replacement value, we'll say so honestly rather than pushing a repair that won't hold up long term.

Annual maintenance extends a boiler's service life significantly. Bleeding radiators to remove trapped air, checking system pressure, and flushing sediment from the system are all things worth doing before heating season starts, not after a problem shows up. A well-maintained residential boiler can reasonably run for 15 to 20 years, while one that's never been serviced often needs major repairs well before that.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do you work on older boiler systems?

Yes, we regularly service the residential boiler systems still common in Kent's older housing stock.

Why is my boiler losing pressure?

A slow leak somewhere in the system is the most common cause. We'll trace it to the source before recommending a repair.

Is it worth repairing an old boiler?

Often yes, since most individual repairs cost far less than a full replacement. We'll give you an honest comparison based on your specific unit's age and condition.

Why are some of my radiators cold?

This is usually trapped air in the system, which is typically resolved by bleeding the radiators and checking circulation.

How often should a boiler be serviced?

An annual check before heating season, covering pressure, circulation, and sediment, is a reasonable schedule for most residential systems and catches small issues before they become larger repairs.

Can a boiler leak be dangerous?

A water leak itself usually isn't a safety hazard, though it should be addressed before it damages surrounding flooring or framing. A suspected gas smell near a gas-fired unit is a different situation and should be treated as an emergency.
