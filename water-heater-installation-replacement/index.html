Overview

Water Heater Replacement For Kent Homes

Most water heaters last 8 to 12 years before the tank itself starts to fail, and Kent has a real mix of housing ages that puts many original units in that window right now. Older valley-floor homes from the 1950s and 60s are frequently on their second or third replacement tank, while newer East Hill construction from the 1990s and 2000s is starting to reach the age where the original unit needs attention.

King County's municipal water supply is generally soft compared to many parts of the country, which is easier on tank longevity than the hard-water regions common elsewhere. That doesn't mean a tank lasts forever, but it does mean scale buildup usually isn't the reason a Kent water heater fails early. More often it's simple age, a slow tank leak, or a unit that was undersized for the household from the start.

Signs You Need This

Signs Your Water Heater Needs Replacing

Lukewarm Water, Not Hot

A weakening heating element or a failing thermostat often shows up first as water that never gets fully hot, rather than a total loss of hot water.

Rusty Or Discolored Water

Rust-colored water from the hot tap specifically, not the cold, usually means the tank itself is corroding from the inside.

Popping Or Rumbling Noises

Sediment settling at the bottom of an older tank causes a popping sound during heating, and it's a sign the tank is nearing the end of its service life.

Visible Moisture Or A Puddle

Any moisture around the base of the tank, even a small amount, means the tank is leaking and needs replacement, not repair.

Safety First

Before We Arrive

Shut Off The Cold Water Valve

If the tank is actively leaking, turn the cold water supply valve at the top of the tank clockwise to stop feeding it.

Cut Power Or Gas To The Unit

For an electric tank, switch off the breaker. For gas, turn the gas control valve to the off position rather than attempting to relight the pilot yourself.

Move Nearby Items Away

Clear boxes, flooring materials, or anything water-sensitive away from the base of the tank while you wait.

Don't Wait On A Rotten-Egg Smell

If you smell gas near the unit, leave the area and call your gas utility's emergency line before calling us.

How We Fix It

Tank, Tankless, Or Heat Pump

A standard tank replacement is the fastest and least expensive option, and it's usually the right call when you're replacing a failed unit with a similar model. Tankless units cost more upfront but heat water on demand rather than storing it, which means an endless hot water supply and a smaller physical footprint. The trade-off is a more involved installation if your home doesn't already have the venting and gas or electrical capacity for it.

Heat pump water heaters are worth a real look for a Kent replacement. They use a fraction of the electricity of a standard electric tank, and there are genuine Washington state and utility rebate programs tied to electrification that can offset a meaningful part of the upfront cost. They do need more clearance space and produce cool exhaust air, so they're not the right fit for every mechanical room, but for the right space they can meaningfully lower a household's energy bill over the unit's lifespan.

FAQs

Common Questions

How fast can you install a new water heater?

Most straightforward tank replacements are completed the same day for calls placed before early afternoon. Tankless or heat pump installs that need extra electrical or venting work sometimes take a bit longer.

Do you haul away the old tank?

Yes, removal and disposal of the old unit is included in every replacement.

Is a tankless water heater worth it for a Kent home?

It depends on your household's hot water use and your home's existing gas or electrical capacity. We'll give you an honest comparison for your specific situation rather than a blanket recommendation.

Are there rebates for a heat pump water heater in Washington?

Washington and several local utilities offer real rebate programs tied to electrification upgrades, including heat pump water heaters. Program details and amounts change, so we'll point you to the current utility program when we quote the job.
