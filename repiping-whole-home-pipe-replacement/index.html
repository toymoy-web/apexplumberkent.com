Overview

When A Repair Isn't Enough Anymore

Repiping means replacing a home's supply lines entirely, usually with modern PEX or copper, rather than patching the failure of the week. It's a bigger job than a single repair, and most homeowners only look into it after they've already paid for two or three separate leak repairs and started to suspect the real problem is the pipe material, not any one spot in it.

This comes up more in Kent than in newer suburbs specifically because of the housing stock. Homes built on the valley floor through the 1950s and 60s commonly still run original galvanized steel supply lines. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out over 40 to 60 years. Once it starts, new leaks tend to show up in different places every few months rather than staying in one spot, which is the exact pattern that makes a repair-by-repair approach a losing bet.

Signs You Need This

Signs Your Pipes Are The Real Problem

Brown Or Rusty Water

Discolored water from multiple fixtures, not just one, usually points to internal corrosion in the supply lines themselves.

Leaks In Different Spots Over Time

A second or third unrelated leak within a couple of years is a pattern, not bad luck, especially in an older galvanized system.

Low Water Pressure Throughout The House

Corrosion narrows the inside of a galvanized pipe over time, which shows up as weak pressure at every fixture, not just one.

Visible Corrosion On Exposed Pipe

Flaking, rust-colored buildup on any exposed section of pipe in a basement or crawlspace is worth having checked even before a leak happens.

What This Usually Means

Multiple Leaks Usually Mean The Pipe, Not The Spot

If you're searching for a plumber because you've already had more than one leak, the instinct is to think you've just had bad luck twice. In galvanized-pipe homes, it's rarely luck. Once corrosion starts inside the pipe wall, it doesn't stay in one place, and each new leak is the same underlying problem showing up somewhere new. A repipe fixes the actual cause instead of buying another year before the next leak.

Safety First

Before We Arrive

Know Your Main Shutoff Location

Find your home's main water shutoff valve ahead of time in case a leak worsens before the scheduled work.

Clear Access Points

Clear a path to crawlspaces, attics, or under-sink areas where supply lines run, so the assessment can move quickly.

Plan For Short Water Interruptions

The job is scheduled in sections, but expect brief periods without water on the day of active work.

Move Stored Items Away From Work Areas

Clear boxes or stored items from basements or crawlspaces near pipe runs before the crew arrives.

How We Fix It

How A Repipe Actually Works

We start with an on-site assessment, checking accessible pipe sections and fixture pressure to confirm whether a repipe is genuinely the right call, or whether a more targeted repair is still reasonable for your situation. We won't recommend a full repipe if a repair still makes sense; that's a bigger job and a bigger cost, and we're not going to push it if it isn't warranted.

When a repipe is the right call, most Kent homes are good candidates for PEX supply lines, which resist corrosion completely and flex slightly during a freeze rather than splitting the way rigid pipe can. Both PEX and copper installations meet the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Washington has adopted statewide. The job is scheduled in sections so your household keeps water access through most of the process, and it's genuinely disruptive for a few days, which is why we walk through the full timeline with you before booking anything.

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if I need a repipe instead of a repair?

A free on-site assessment is the honest way to answer this. Multiple unrelated leaks, brown water from more than one fixture, and low pressure throughout the house are all signs worth having checked.

Will I have water during the repipe?

The job is scheduled in sections specifically so your household keeps access to water through most of the process, with only short planned interruptions.

Is PEX pipe actually reliable?

Yes. PEX has been used in residential plumbing for decades, resists the corrosion that fails galvanized pipe, and handles minor freezing better than rigid copper.

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

It depends on the home's size and how accessible the existing pipe runs are. We'll give you a specific timeline as part of the on-site assessment, not a generic estimate.
