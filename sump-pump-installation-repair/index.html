Overview

Keeping Your Sump Pump Working

When a sump pump stops working, water is usually on the floor within hours of heavy rain. Most of our sump pump work in Kent is repair: stuck float switches, failed check valves, burned-out motors, and dead battery backups. When a pump is too old or too small to be worth repairing, we replace it and size the new system to the water it actually has to move.

A sump pump moves groundwater that collects around a home's foundation out and away before it can seep into a basement or crawlspace. It's a genuinely relevant system in Kent, given the valley floor's flat, low-lying geography and real groundwater intrusion risk documented by King County's own flood control district.

A failed sump pump usually shows up at the worst possible time, during heavy rain, which is exactly when you need it working. We repair existing systems first, install new ones where there is none, and add battery backup for homes that lose power during storms.

Repairs

Sump Pump Repairs We Handle

Stuck Or Failed Float Switch

When the float hangs up on the basin wall or fails, the pump either never starts or never stops. It is often a quick adjustment or a switch swap.

Failed Check Valve

A bad check valve lets water drain back into the basin after every cycle, so the pump short-cycles and wears out early.

Burned-Out Motor Or Jammed Impeller

A pump that hums without moving water, or trips the breaker, may have debris in the impeller or a motor at the end of its life.

Dead Battery Backup

Backup batteries wear out quietly, and a dead one only shows up during the power outage you installed it for. We test and replace them.

Kent Context

Where Kent Sump Pumps Matter Most

Sump pumps matter most on Kent's valley floor, close to the Green River and in the low-lying parts of the valley around downtown, where groundwater sits high through the wet months. Hillside homes on East Hill, West Hill, and Scenic Hill need them less often, usually when a daylight basement or a history of water after storms points to a drainage problem.

Signs You Need This

Signs Your Sump Pump Needs Repair

Water In The Basement Or Crawlspace

Any recurring water after rain is a sign the current drainage setup, or lack of one, isn't handling groundwater properly.

Pump Runs Constantly

A pump that never seems to shut off may have a stuck switch or be undersized for the actual water volume.

Pump Doesn't Turn On At All

A failed pump won't help during the next storm. This needs to be caught before it's actually needed.

No Backup Power Source

A power outage during a storm is exactly when a sump pump is needed most, and a battery backup covers that gap.

What This Usually Means

Basement Water After Rain Usually Means Your Drainage Needs Help

If you're dealing with water in a basement or crawlspace after a storm, the instinct might be to look for the leak. Often there isn't one. It's groundwater finding its way in around the foundation, and the actual fix is managing that water before it gets inside, which is exactly what a sump pump system does.

Safety First

Emergency Steps Before We Arrive

Stay Out Of Standing Water Near Outlets

Don't wade through basement water near electrical outlets or a plugged-in pump. Shut off power to the area at the breaker if you can do so safely.

Move Stored Items Off The Floor

Get boxes and belongings up off a wet basement or crawlspace floor before the visit.

Note When The Water Started

Knowing whether it appeared during or after a specific storm helps us diagnose whether it's a pump failure or a new drainage issue.

Don't Ignore A Tripped GFCI Outlet

If the outlet powering your pump keeps tripping, that's worth mentioning right away rather than repeatedly resetting it.

How We Fix It

Repair And Installation

For a new installation, we size the pump to your home's actual water volume and install a proper basin, check valve, and discharge line routed well away from the foundation. For an existing system, we test the pump, float switch, and check valve to find the actual point of failure.

Battery backup systems keep the pump running during a power outage, which is exactly when storm-driven groundwater is most likely to be a problem in the first place. We test the backup system's charge and function as part of any new installation, not just the primary pump.

Emergency Response

Emergency Sump Pump Failure In Kent

A sump pump that has stopped, a pit that is full and not draining, or a basement or crawl space taking on water is an emergency, especially during heavy rain. Keep belongings and electronics off the floor and call our 24/7 emergency line.

We can diagnose a failed float switch, check valve, or motor on the spot, and fit a battery backup so the next outage does not repeat the problem.

Related Pump Calls

Sewage Ejector Pumps Are Not Sump Pumps

Basement bathrooms often drain through a sewage ejector pump instead of a sump pump. The two look similar but handle very different water. If yours is failing, tell us when you call so we bring the right parts.
