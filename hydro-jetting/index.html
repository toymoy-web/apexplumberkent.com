Overview

A Deeper Clean Than Cabling

A standard cable auger clears a clog by punching a hole through whatever's blocking the pipe. That works for a lot of calls, but it leaves the rest of the buildup, grease coating, root hairs, mineral scale, stuck to the pipe walls. Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water stream to scour the full diameter of the pipe clean, which is why it's the real fix for a line that keeps clogging in the same spot no matter how many times it's snaked.

This shows up often in Kent's older neighborhoods, where mature street trees are common near clay and cast iron sewer laterals from the 1950s and 60s. Root hairs find their way into pipe joints looking for moisture, and a cable can cut through the immediate blockage without touching the smaller roots still lining the pipe, which regrow within a few months.

Signs You Need This

When Jetting Is The Right Call

The Same Line Clogs Repeatedly

If cabling clears it but the same spot backs up again within a few months, roots or heavy grease buildup are the likely cause.

Multiple Drains Are Slow At Once

A main line partially blocked with buildup often shows up as several fixtures draining slowly, not just one.

A Camera Inspection Shows Buildup Or Roots

When we run a camera and see grease coating or root intrusion along the pipe, jetting is the fix that actually clears it rather than punching through it.

Commercial Kitchen Line Grease Buildup

Kitchens with heavy grease output need periodic jetting as maintenance, not just when a clog has already formed.

What This Usually Means

If Snaking Never Fully Fixes It, You Probably Need Jetting

A lot of calls we get start with someone who's already had a drain snaked once or twice, and it keeps coming back. That pattern is the clearest sign that cabling isn't reaching the actual problem, whether that's root intrusion or a thick grease coating lining the pipe. Jetting clears the entire pipe wall rather than just opening a path through the blockage, which is why it holds up longer on a recurring clog.

Safety First

Before We Arrive

Skip The Rental Equipment

Consumer-grade jetting rental units on a pipe of unknown condition can worsen a hidden crack. Get a camera check first if you're unsure.

Clear Interior Cleanout Access

If your main cleanout is inside (basement, garage, or utility closet), clear a path to it before we arrive.

Keep Pets And Kids Clear Of Exterior Access Points

Outdoor cleanouts stay open and in use during the service, so keep the area clear while we work.

Mention Any Recent Sewer Line Work

Let us know about any past repairs to the line so we can adjust water pressure accordingly.

How We Fix It

How We Jet A Line Safely

We run a camera inspection first on any line that's a repeat offender, both to confirm jetting is the right tool and to check the pipe's condition. High-pressure water is genuinely effective on pipe that's structurally sound, but it isn't the right choice for a line that's already cracked or badly deteriorated, and we'll tell you honestly if that's what the camera shows.

The jetting hose feeds a rotating nozzle through the line that scours grease, scale, and root material from the full pipe wall, not just the center of the blockage. For a line with heavier root intrusion, we sometimes recommend a repeat jetting on a maintenance schedule rather than a single visit, especially if the trees causing the intrusion aren't going anywhere.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

For clay, cast iron, and PVC lines in sound structural condition, yes. If a camera inspection shows a pipe that's already cracked or significantly deteriorated, we'll recommend a different approach instead.

How is jetting different from snaking?

A cable clears a path through a clog. Jetting scours the entire inside diameter of the pipe clean with high-pressure water, which is why it holds up better against a clog that keeps returning.

Do I need a camera inspection before jetting?

For a first-time clog, not always. For a recurring clog in the same spot, yes, so we can confirm the actual cause and check that the pipe is a good candidate for jetting.

Can jetting damage my pipes?

Used correctly on a structurally sound pipe, no. That's exactly why we check the line's condition first on any repeat clog rather than jetting blind.
