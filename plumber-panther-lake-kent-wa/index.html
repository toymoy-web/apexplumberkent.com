Safety First

If Water Is Running Or Leaking

Find Your Shutoff

Shut off the main water valve if you have an active leak. It is usually near where the water line enters the house or at the meter box.

Keep Away From Standing Water Near Power

Don't walk through standing water near outlets or a panel. Shut off power to that area at the breaker only if it is safe to reach.

Clear The Area

Move belongings away from the water and keep people out of any area where water is near electrical outlets or ceiling fixtures.

Local Knowledge

About The Panther Lake Area

Panther Lake was formerly an unincorporated part of King County. Residents voted on the annexation on November 3, 2009, and it took effect on July 1, 2010, according to Kent city history and local news coverage. Kent Reporter articles at the time described an area of about 5 square miles and nearly 24,000 residents.

Those articles described the area as running east of 104th Avenue Southeast to near 132nd Avenue Southeast, and from near Southeast 192nd Street to north of Southeast 236th or 240th Street. Soos Creek Park sits just east of 132nd Avenue Southeast. These are the boundaries as reported then, so call with your address if you are near an edge.

Seven Kent School District schools served the area before annexation, including Kentridge High School and six elementary schools. Panther Lake Elementary shares the area's name, which is one reason the name stuck with residents. The reporting did not name separate neighborhoods inside the area, so we treat Panther Lake as one locality.

What The Annexation Means For Plumbing Calls

A Large Area With Many Streets

Which Agency Serves You

After an annexation, some residents are unsure which agency handles water and sewer. Your bill names your provider, and that provider can confirm its service area.

Edges Of The Area

Panther Lake meets East Hill and Fairwood. If you live near an edge, call with your address and we will confirm coverage.

Homes Of Different Ages

Housing across a five square mile area is varied. A short inspection tells us more than the age of the street.

Helpful Notes

Practical Plumbing Notes

If a toilet or tub drain backs up, stop using water in that part of the house and call. Repeated backups often point to a problem in the main line and not the fixture, and a camera inspection can show where the trouble is.

Water heaters are among the most common calls anywhere. If yours is leaking, rusty or slow to heat, shut off the supply and call. Whether to repair or replace depends on the tank's condition and what the repair would cost.

A sudden rise in your water bill with no change in use can mean a hidden leak. Check that all fixtures are off and look at your meter. If it still moves, call for leak detection.
