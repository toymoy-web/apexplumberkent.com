Know Your Main Shutoff

Most Covington homes have a main water shutoff in the basement, crawlspace, or garage. Find it before you need it.

Turn Off A Failing Water Heater

For an electric unit, switch off the breaker. For gas, turn the gas control valve to off rather than trying to relight the pilot yourself.

Local Knowledge

Slow Drain Or No Hot Water In Covington? Here's What We See Most

Covington was incorporated in 1997, and a large share of its housing dates to the growth that followed, subdivisions built through the late 1990s and 2000s around neighborhoods like East Hill, Meridian Heights, and Cherokee Bay. That makes Covington's plumbing profile noticeably younger than Kent's own valley-floor housing stock, but modern construction still runs into modern problems: PEX and copper supply lines that are now old enough for fixtures and water heaters to start failing on schedule.

The most common calls we get from Covington homes cluster around two things: drain clogs in kitchens and bathrooms built with standard plastic drain lines, and water heater failures in aging units. Most of these units are now 10 to 15 years old, right in the window where a tank starts to go. Covington is a low-walkability, car-dependent suburb, which also means most homes here are single-family with full basements or crawlspaces, giving us straightforward access for most repairs.

A typical Covington visit is quicker to resolve than calls from our older-housing coverage areas. Most homes here share similar construction: full or daylight basements, standard PVC drain lines, and supply lines that are old enough to show wear but young enough that a full repipe is rarely the actual answer.

Our drain cleaning and water heater technicians dispatch to every address inside ZIP 98042, whether that's a home near Covington Community Park or a townhouse closer to the Kent Kangley Road corridor.
