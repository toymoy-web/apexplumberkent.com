Toilet Repair & Installation in Kent, WA

A running toilet wastes real water every single day, and it's almost always a small, inexpensive part causing it. A wobbly or leaking one is usually just as quick and inexpensive to fix properly.

Overview

Repairing Or Replacing A Toilet

A toilet that runs continuously almost always comes down to a worn flapper or an improperly adjusted fill valve, both genuinely inexpensive parts to source and replace. A toilet that rocks or leaks at the base usually means the wax ring seal underneath has failed and needs replacing.

When a toilet is old enough, or damaged enough, that repair doesn't make sense, we handle full replacement and installation, including confirming the new unit fits your existing rough-in measurements correctly.

Kent Context

Toilets Across Kent

Running toilets, weak flushes, and failed fill valves are among our quickest Kent calls, in East Hill ramblers and West Hill townhomes alike. Most repairs are a flapper, fill valve, or wax ring, and we replace the toilet only when the porcelain or the age makes repair pointless.

Signs You Need This

Signs Your Toilet Needs Attention

Running Continuously

A worn flapper or a fill valve that's not shutting off completely is the most common cause, and it's an easy fix.

Rocking Or Wobbling

A toilet that moves at the base often has a broken wax ring seal or loose mounting bolts underneath.

Leaking At The Base

Water pooling around the base points to a failed wax ring seal that needs to be replaced.

Weak Or Incomplete Flush

A partial or weak flush can mean a clogged trapway or a worn flush valve, depending on the specific symptom.

Safety First

Before We Arrive

Know Your Shutoff Valve

The small valve behind or below the toilet stops water to that fixture specifically without shutting off the whole house.

Avoid Chemical Drop-In Tank Tablets

Some cleaning tablets can degrade rubber seals and flapper valves over time, contributing to the exact leaks we're often called to fix.

Don't Force A Stuck Shutoff Valve

An old valve that won't turn can snap if forced. Leave it alone and let us handle it if it's seized.

Keep The Area Clear

Clear items stored around the base of the toilet so we have room to work.

How We Fix It

Repair Or Full Replacement

For most running or weak-flush complaints, we replace the specific worn internal part and test the flush before finishing. For a rocking or leaking toilet, we pull the unit, replace the wax ring seal, and reset it properly and level.

For a full replacement, we confirm your home's existing rough-in measurement so the new toilet fits correctly, and we haul away the old unit as part of the installation.

Older toilets, especially units installed before the mid-1990s, can genuinely use more than twice the water per flush of a modern model. If yours is one of those older units, a replacement often pays for itself over time in reduced water use, on top of resolving whatever repair issue prompted the call in the first place.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my toilet keep running?

This is almost always a worn flapper or a fill valve that isn't shutting off fully, both inexpensive and quick to replace.

Is a rocking toilet a big problem?

It's worth fixing promptly, since a broken wax ring seal underneath can eventually lead to water damage on the floor below.

Can you install a new toilet I've already bought?

Yes, we can install a toilet you've purchased, as long as it fits your home's existing rough-in measurement.

How much water does a running toilet waste?

It can be a genuinely significant amount over time, which is exactly why it's worth fixing rather than living with it.

Can a toilet be repaired if parts aren't sold anymore?

For most standard toilets, generic replacement parts fit regardless of brand or age. A genuinely obsolete or specialty model is one of the few cases where replacement becomes the more practical option.

Why does my toilet make a phantom flush sound?

A phantom flush, where the tank refills briefly on its own, is a classic sign of a slow leak through the flapper valve, and it's usually an inexpensive fix.

Do you install comfort-height toilets?

Yes, comfort-height and standard-height models are both available, and we can help you decide which fits your household's needs.

Can a running toilet cause a higher water bill?

Yes, sometimes significantly. It's one of the most overlooked causes of an unexpectedly high water bill in an otherwise unremarkable month.
