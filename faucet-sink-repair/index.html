Overview

Fixing Drips, Leaks, And Worn Fixtures

Most faucet drips come down to a worn cartridge, O-ring, or valve seat, all inexpensive parts that resolve the problem completely once replaced. A faucet that's leaking at the base, rather than the spout, usually points to a different seal that's failed and needs replacing.

Sink repairs cover a wider range, from a slow drain at the fixture itself to a loose or cracked basin. We diagnose the actual issue rather than assuming a full fixture replacement is needed when a part swap will do.

Signs You Need This

Signs Your Faucet Or Sink Needs Attention

Constant Dripping

A worn cartridge or O-ring is the most common cause, and it's usually a straightforward repair.

Leaking At The Base

Water pooling around the base of the faucet, rather than dripping from the spout, points to a different seal needing replacement.

Low Or Inconsistent Water Flow

A clogged aerator or a failing cartridge can both cause weak or sputtering flow.

Loose Or Wobbly Faucet

A faucet that moves at the base often has loose mounting hardware that's straightforward to tighten or replace.

How We Fix It

Repair Or Replacement

For most drips and leaks, we identify and replace the specific worn part, cartridge, O-ring, or valve seat, rather than replacing the whole fixture. This keeps the cost down when a full replacement genuinely isn't needed.

When a fixture is old enough that parts are hard to source, or the finish is failing along with the internals, we'll walk you through replacement options that genuinely fit your sink's existing setup and layout.

A clogged aerator is genuinely worth checking before assuming a bigger problem exists. Mineral and debris buildup inside the small screen at a faucet's tip can cause weak or sputtering flow that looks like a cartridge failure but is actually a five-minute cleaning fix. We check this first on any low-flow complaint before recommending any parts replacement.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my faucet keep dripping after I tightened it?

Tightening the handle doesn't fix a worn cartridge or O-ring, which is almost always the actual cause of a persistent drip.

Can you match my existing fixture style?

In many cases, yes, or we can help you choose a compatible replacement if the exact part isn't available anymore.

Is a dripping faucet worth fixing right away?

Yes. A slow drip adds up in wasted water and utility cost over time, and it's usually an inexpensive fix.

Do you repair kitchen and bathroom fixtures both?

Yes, we handle faucet and sink repair throughout the house.

Should I replace a fixture myself to save money?

A basic cartridge swap is sometimes a reasonable DIY job, but a misdiagnosed leak or a stripped fitting can turn a small repair into a bigger one, so we're happy to take a look first if you're unsure.

What causes a sink to drain slowly but not fully clog?

A partial buildup of soap scum, hair, or grease inside the trap is the most common cause, and it usually clears with a straightforward cleaning rather than a full drain service.

Do you fix touchless or smart faucets?

Yes, though sensor and battery components sometimes need parts specific to that model, which we'll confirm before starting the repair.
