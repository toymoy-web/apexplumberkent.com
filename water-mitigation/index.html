Overview

What Mitigation Actually Means

Water mitigation and water damage restoration are related, but they're not the same job, and the distinction actually matters. Mitigation is the immediate, first-response work done in the hours after a leak, flood, or burst pipe: extracting standing water, setting up drying equipment, and taking steps to stop the damage from spreading any further into the structure. Restoration is the fuller process that follows, repairing and rebuilding whatever the water actually damaged once the site is dry and stable.

The faster mitigation starts after a water event, the smaller the eventual restoration job tends to be. Water that sits for six hours does meaningfully less damage than water that sits for two days, which is exactly why this is treated as a 24/7 emergency line rather than something scheduled during business hours.

Kent Context

Fast Response On The Valley Floor

Mitigation speed matters most in Kent's low-lying valley-floor neighborhoods and downtown buildings, where water can reach floors and drywall quickly and groundwater can complicate drying. Hillside homes on East Hill and West Hill are more often dealing with a burst pipe or appliance failure. Either way, extraction and drying start as soon as we arrive.

Safety First

Emergency Steps Before We Arrive

Stop The Source If It's Safe

If the water is coming from a visible, reachable shutoff, turning it off can limit how much material gets wet before mitigation starts.

Cut Power To Standing Water Areas

Don't walk through standing water near outlets or electrical panels. Shut off power to that area at the breaker if you can do so without stepping in water.

Move Valuables Off The Wet Floor

Get furniture, electronics, and anything irreplaceable up off a wet floor or out of the affected room while you wait.

Photograph The Damage First

Take photos and video before anything is moved or cleaned up. It genuinely helps with an insurance claim, whatever your coverage turns out to be.

How We Fix It

What Happens During Mitigation

The first step is stopping any active water source where that's safely possible, followed by extracting standing water with pumps and wet vacuums.

From there, we set up industrial air movers and dehumidifiers and use moisture meters to map exactly which materials, flooring, drywall, subfloor, are still holding water, since materials can hold moisture well after they look dry on the surface. This process generally follows the IICRC S500 water damage mitigation and restoration standard, the published industry reference for structural drying.

Water is classified by source as part of this process: clean water from a supply line is the most straightforward to mitigate, while water that's been sitting or came from a sewer backup is treated as contaminated and handled with additional precautions. We'll tell you honestly which category applies to your situation, since it affects both the mitigation approach and what happens next in the restoration phase.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is emergency water mitigation available in Kent?

Yes. Our 24/7 line, (253) 294-7284, handles emergency water mitigation across Kent and the surrounding area.

Is water mitigation the same as water damage restoration?

They're related but distinct. Mitigation is the immediate first response, stopping the damage from spreading. Restoration is the fuller repair and rebuild that follows once the site is dry and stable.

How fast should mitigation start after a water event?

As soon as possible. Water that sits for even a day causes meaningfully more damage than water addressed within a few hours, which is why this line runs 24/7.

Do I need both mitigation and restoration?

For most real water events, yes. Mitigation limits the damage first, and restoration repairs what was actually affected. We handle both, often as a continuous process rather than two separate calls.

Is this different from a plumbing repair?

Yes. A plumbing repair fixes what caused the water event. Mitigation and restoration address the water damage itself, and both run on this dedicated line, separate from our plumbing dispatch number.
