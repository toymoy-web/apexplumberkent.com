Overview

What A Slab Leak Actually Is

A slab leak happens when a water line running underneath a home's concrete foundation develops a leak. Because the line is buried in the slab itself, the leak isn't visible the way a leak under a sink would be. It often shows up first as a warm floor patch, a higher water bill, or the sound of running water when nothing's on.

Left alone, a slab leak can undermine the soil beneath the foundation over time and cause real structural concern, on top of the ongoing water waste. Catching it early keeps both the repair and the water damage smaller.

Signs You Need This

Signs Of A Slab Leak

Warm Spot On The Floor

A patch of floor that's noticeably warmer than the rest, especially over a hot water line, is a classic slab leak sign.

Sound Of Running Water

Hearing water running when every fixture in the house is off points to water escaping somewhere in the system.

Unexplained Water Bill Increase

A slab leak can run for weeks before it's visible anywhere, showing up first as a jump in your water bill.

Cracks In Flooring Or Walls

Foundation movement from a long-running slab leak can eventually show up as new cracks in flooring or nearby walls.

What This Usually Means

A Warm Floor Patch Is A Specific Sign, Not A Coincidence

If you've noticed one spot on your floor that's warmer than the rest, that's not a random quirk of your house. It's a strong sign of a hot water line leaking underneath the slab at that exact location. Combined with a rising water bill or the sound of running water, it's worth having checked before the leak has a chance to affect the foundation area.

Safety First

Before We Arrive

Don't Cut Into The Slab Yourself

Attempting to expose the line without detection equipment risks striking electrical or other buried lines, and it rarely finds the actual leak.

Note Where The Warm Spot Is

Mark or photograph the exact location and how large the warm area is. It helps narrow the search before we even arrive.

Track Your Water Meter

Note a reading and check it again after a few hours with everything off, to help confirm the leak's severity.

Watch For New Cracks

If you notice a new crack in flooring or a nearby wall, mention it right away since it can affect how urgently the repair should happen.

How We Fix It

Finding And Fixing The Leak

We use acoustic detection to listen for the specific sound of pressurized water escaping under the slab, combined with thermal imaging to trace the warm water line's path. This narrows the leak's location closely before any concrete is touched.

Repair options depend on the leak's location and the home's plumbing layout, ranging from a targeted slab penetration to rerouting the line above the floor instead of repairing the buried section directly. We'll walk you through the option that makes sense for your specific situation and your home's layout. Any repair follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Washington has adopted statewide.

FAQs

Common Questions

How serious is a slab leak?

It's worth addressing promptly. Left alone, it can affect the soil under the foundation and cause real structural concern over time, on top of ongoing water waste.

Do you have to break up my whole floor?

No. Acoustic and thermal detection let us narrow the leak's location closely, so the repair opening is small and targeted rather than a large section of slab.

Can a slab leak be rerouted instead of repaired directly?

In many cases, yes. Rerouting the line above the slab avoids disturbing the foundation at all, depending on your home's layout.

Will insurance cover this?

Sudden slab leaks are often covered, though specifics vary by policy. We recommend confirming your coverage with your insurer.
