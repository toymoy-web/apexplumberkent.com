Garbage Disposal Humming But Not Spinning? A Black Diamond, WA Guide

A humming disposal almost always means the motor is fine and something is physically stuck. Here's what to check first.

Written by the Kent Apex Plumbing team Published September 2026 Updated October 2026

A garbage disposal that hums when you flip the switch but doesn't actually spin is one of the more reassuring sounds a broken appliance can make, even though it doesn't feel that way in the moment. The humming means the motor is receiving power and trying to run. Something is just physically blocking the blades from turning, which is a very different, and usually much cheaper, problem than a dead motor.

What's Actually Jamming It

Fibrous foods like celery, corn husks, and onion skins are common culprits, since their fibers can wrap around the spinning mechanism rather than getting cut cleanly. Hard objects that accidentally fall into the disposal, a piece of silverware, a bottle cap, a stray bone, can also physically wedge against the blades and stop them from turning. Occasionally it's simply an accumulation of smaller debris that's built up enough to create resistance the motor can't overcome on its own.

The Reset Button Most People Forget

Most garbage disposals have a small red or black reset button on the underside of the unit that trips automatically when the motor overheats or overloads, similar to a circuit breaker. If your disposal isn't humming at all, completely silent when you flip the switch, this reset button is the first thing worth checking before assuming the worst. Press it firmly, and if it clicks back into place, try the switch again.

How To Safely Clear A Jam Yourself

Turn Off Power At The Switch And Breaker First

Never put your hand into a disposal that could still receive power, even briefly.

Use The Hex Wrench Opening, Not Your Hand

Most disposals have a hex-shaped opening underneath for a manual crank wrench, which lets you rotate the blades manually to work a jam loose.

Look For A Visible Obstruction From Above

With the power off, a flashlight and a pair of tongs, never your fingers, can sometimes remove a visible object.

Press The Reset Button After Clearing The Jam

Once the obstruction is cleared, reset the unit before testing it again at the switch.

When It's Genuinely Time For A Replacement

If the unit hums but a manual crank at the hex opening doesn't turn the blades at all, or if you've cleared an obstruction but the humming continues without the blades engaging, the motor or a mechanical component inside has likely failed.

In that case, there isn't a simple jam to clear. Similarly, a disposal that's leaking from the bottom has a failed seal that isn't something you repair, since the unit needs to come off entirely to replace it, at which point installing a new unit is generally the more sensible option.

Our garbage disposal repair and installation service handles both situations directly, clearing a genuine mechanical jam when that's the actual issue, or properly sizing and installing a replacement when the old unit has reached the end of its service life.

If your kitchen sink is also draining slowly alongside the disposal issue, that's frequently a related but separate problem, and our drain cleaning service addresses the actual clog in the line rather than assuming the disposal itself is the sole cause.

Habits That Prevent The Next Jam

Running cold water during and briefly after using the disposal helps solidify any grease so it clears the line rather than coating it, and it's a simple habit that meaningfully reduces how often jams occur.

Cutting fibrous scraps into smaller pieces before putting them down the disposal, rather than feeding in whole stalks or husks, also reduces the chance of a wrap-around jam. Neither habit fixes a unit that's already failing, but both genuinely extend the working life of a disposal that's currently healthy.

What To Avoid Putting Down A Disposal Entirely

Some materials cause problems no matter how careful you are otherwise. Grease and cooking oil should never go down a disposal at all, since they solidify in the line regardless of how much cold water you run afterward.

Coffee grounds and eggshells are commonly believed to sharpen disposal blades, but they actually tend to accumulate in the trap and contribute to slow drains over time rather than helping anything. Starchy foods like pasta and rice expand when they absorb water, and putting a large quantity down a disposal at once can create a paste-like clog that's harder to clear than a typical jam.

If you've been putting any of these down your disposal regularly and you're now dealing with frequent jams or a slow-draining sink, it's worth addressing both issues together. Fixing the disposal habit and the drain condition at the same time tends to solve the problem more completely than fixing just one or the other.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is it safe to reach into my disposal to check for a jam?

Only with the power fully off at both the switch and the breaker, and even then, using tongs rather than your hand is the safer approach.

Why does my disposal keep tripping the reset button?

A recurring trip usually means something is consistently overloading the motor, which is worth having checked rather than repeatedly resetting it as a permanent solution.

How long do garbage disposals typically last?

Most units last 8 to 12 years with normal use, though heavy daily use or frequent jams can shorten that lifespan.

Is a louder-than-usual disposal a sign of a problem?

Often yes, especially a sudden change in sound. It can indicate a loose component or something lodged against the blades that hasn't fully jammed the motor yet.

Can I upgrade to a more powerful disposal without changing the plumbing?

In most cases yes, since disposals are generally designed to fit standard connections. We can confirm compatibility with your existing setup during installation.

Is a garbage disposal a problem for septic systems in Black Diamond?

King County says garbage disposal use can change how often a septic tank needs pumping, so a disposal adds load. Homes around Lake Sawyer are on private septic, so ask your certified maintainer how a disposal fits your tank size.
