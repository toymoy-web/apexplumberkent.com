Is This a Real Plumbing Emergency? A Kent, WA Homeowner Guide

Not every plumbing problem needs a call at 2am. Here's an honest way to sort out what's genuinely urgent from what can reasonably wait.

Written by the Kent Apex Plumbing team Published September 2026 Updated October 2026

One of the more common questions we get isn't about a specific plumbing problem at all. It's whether the problem someone's dealing with actually counts as an emergency, or whether it's reasonable to wait until regular business hours. It's a fair question, and the honest answer depends on a few specific factors rather than a blanket rule either way.

What Actually Counts As An Emergency

Active Water You Can't Stop

A burst pipe or a supply line that's actively flooding a room, especially if you can't locate or operate the shutoff valve, is a genuine emergency.

Sewage Backing Up Into The House

This is both a health concern and a property damage risk that shouldn't wait, regardless of the hour.

No Water At All To The Entire House

A complete loss of water, rather than an issue with one fixture, often points to a main line problem worth addressing right away.

A Gas Smell Near Any Appliance

This isn't a plumbing emergency specifically, but it's a genuine emergency. Leave the area and contact your gas utility immediately.

What Can Reasonably Wait

A slow drain, a running toilet, a dripping faucet, or a water heater that's producing lukewarm rather than hot water are all real problems worth addressing, but none of them typically require a middle-of-the-night call. These issues develop gradually and rarely get dramatically worse overnight, which means a scheduled appointment during normal hours usually serves you just as well as an emergency dispatch, at a lower cost.

The same is generally true for a single slow-to-drain fixture, as opposed to multiple drains backing up at once, which is more often a sign of a main line issue that deserves more urgent attention. The distinction between one slow fixture and a whole-house pattern is often the difference between something that can wait and something that can't.

The Real Cost Difference, Honestly

Emergency, after-hours service typically costs more than a scheduled daytime visit, which is a genuine, practical reason to correctly sort out what actually needs immediate attention versus what can go on the regular schedule. That said, we'd rather you call and describe the situation than try to self-diagnose whether it's urgent enough, especially with water involved, since a problem that seems minor can occasionally be more serious than it first appears.

What To Do While You Decide

If water is actively flowing somewhere it shouldn't, locating and shutting off the nearest fixture valve, or the home's main shutoff if you can't isolate it to one fixture, is the single most useful thing you can do before help arrives, emergency or scheduled. Knowing where that main shutoff valve is before you ever need it is worth five minutes of your time, since searching for it during an actual active leak wastes precious minutes you don't have.

If you're dealing with standing water already, our water damage restoration line is separate from general plumbing dispatch and is answered 24/7 specifically for that kind of situation. For a backup that's affecting the whole house rather than a single fixture, a sewer line issue is the more likely underlying cause, and that's also treated as a same-day priority given how quickly a partial backup can become a full one.

A Few Specific Judgment Calls Worth Naming

A water heater that's stopped producing hot water is inconvenient but rarely urgent, unless it's actively leaking, in which case the leak itself, not the lack of hot water, is what makes it time-sensitive. A clogged toilet is usually not an emergency if it's the only fixture in the house, but it becomes one quickly if it's the only bathroom in the home and there's no other option available to the household in the meantime.

A dishwasher or washing machine that's leaking is worth shutting off at its own supply valve right away, even if the leak seems minor, since a slow drip from an appliance connection can worsen without warning. None of these situations are dangerous to wait on for a few hours in most cases, but knowing the actual shutoff valve for each major appliance ahead of time turns a stressful moment into a much more manageable one.

Local Note

Plumbing Emergencies In Kent: Who To Call For What

If the problem is in the street or at the meter, the City of Kent's water utility handles emergency or general water questions at 253-856-5600. Water escaping inside your home, from a burst pipe or a failed fixture, is on your side of the meter, and that is the call to us. Shut off your main valve first, then call.

After the repair, Kent Customer Services at (253) 856-5201 can tell you whether a leak adjustment applies, which requires the repair to be completed first. We also respond in SeaTac and Des Moines.

FAQs

Common Questions

Should I just call and let you decide if it's an emergency?

Yes, honestly. Describing the situation to us is more reliable than trying to self-diagnose urgency, and we'll tell you plainly whether it needs an emergency dispatch or can be scheduled normally.

Does calling after hours always cost more?

Emergency, after-hours dispatch typically costs more than a scheduled daytime appointment, which is worth knowing when you're deciding whether something can reasonably wait.

What's the single most useful thing to do during an active leak?

Locate and shut off the nearest valve, or the home's main shutoff if needed, before anything else. It limits the damage while help is on the way.

Is it ever wrong to call for emergency service?

No, not from our perspective. We'd genuinely rather take a call that turns out to be less urgent than have someone wait too long on something that needed immediate attention.

Does a home warranty typically cover emergency plumbing calls?

It depends entirely on your specific home warranty policy and its terms. Checking your coverage ahead of time, before an emergency happens, saves confusion in the moment.

Should I turn off my water heater during a plumbing emergency?

If the emergency involves the water heater itself, yes, right away. For an unrelated emergency elsewhere in the house, it's usually not necessary unless instructed otherwise.

Can I get credit on my Kent water bill after an emergency leak?

Possibly. Kent's leak adjustment form requires consumption of double the prior year's average, an owner-signed application within 90 days of the billing period, and a completed repair with receipts. Call Kent Customer Services at (253) 856-5201 to confirm.
