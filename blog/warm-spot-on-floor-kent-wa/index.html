What a Warm Spot on Your Floor Might Mean: A Kent, WA Guide

It's an unusual, specific symptom, and it usually points to one specific cause. Here's what a warm floor patch actually means.

Written by the Kent Apex Plumbing team Published September 2026 Updated October 2026

It's an easy thing to dismiss the first time you notice it, a patch of floor near the kitchen or a hallway that just feels noticeably warmer underfoot than the rest of the house. It's also one of the more specific, genuinely diagnostic symptoms in residential plumbing, and it usually points to one particular cause: a hot water line leaking underneath your home's concrete slab.

Why This Specific Symptom Happens

In homes built on a concrete slab foundation, water supply lines sometimes run underneath the slab itself rather than through interior walls or a crawlspace. If a hot water line develops a leak at that specific location, the escaping hot water warms the surrounding soil and concrete, and that warmth transfers up through the slab to the floor above it.

The warm patch is essentially a heat signature marking the exact location of the leak underneath, which is actually useful information once you know what it means.

Other Signs That Often Show Up Alongside It

A warm spot rarely shows up in complete isolation. Paying attention to what else is happening in the house at the same time often confirms the diagnosis well before any equipment gets involved.

A Rising Water Bill With No Explanation

A slab leak can run for weeks before it's visible anywhere else, showing up first as unexplained water use.

The Sound Of Running Water

Hearing water running when every fixture in the house is off suggests water is escaping somewhere in the system.

Low Water Pressure

A leak diverting water before it reaches your fixtures can cause a noticeable drop in pressure throughout the house.

New Cracks In Flooring Or Nearby Walls

A long-running slab leak can eventually affect the soil beneath the foundation, showing up as cracks over time.

Why It's Worth Addressing Promptly

A slab leak doesn't resolve on its own, and the longer it runs, the more water is wasted and the greater the chance it starts affecting the soil and structure around the foundation. This isn't a symptom to wait out and see if it gets better. It's worth having slab leak detection done as soon as you notice a genuine, consistent warm patch, particularly if it's paired with any of the signs above.

How It's Actually Found And Fixed

Acoustic detection equipment listens for the specific sound pressurized water makes escaping underground, while thermal imaging traces the warm water line's actual path beneath the slab. Together, these narrow the leak's location closely enough that the repair opening in the concrete stays small and targeted, rather than requiring a large section of slab to be broken up on a guess.

Depending on your home's plumbing layout, sometimes the more practical fix is rerouting the line above the slab entirely, avoiding the buried section rather than repairing it directly. Any repair follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Washington has adopted statewide.

If the leak has been running long enough to cause visible water damage, whether that's warped flooring near the warm patch or moisture reaching a nearby wall, that's a separate water damage restoration job on top of the leak repair itself. We'll assess both at the same time so you have a complete picture of the actual scope before any work begins.

What Not To Do Before We Arrive

Don't Cut Into The Slab Yourself

Exposing the line without detection equipment risks striking electrical or other buried lines, and it rarely finds the actual leak location.

Note The Exact Location Of The Warm Patch

Mark or photograph where it is and roughly how large the area is. It genuinely helps narrow the search before we even arrive.

Track Your Water Meter

Note a reading and check it again after a few hours with everything off, to help confirm how significant the leak actually is.

Don't Ignore New Cracks

If you notice a new crack in flooring or a nearby wall, mention it right away, since it can affect how urgently the repair should happen.

Why Homes Built On A Slab Are More Prone To This

Not every home has this risk in the same way. Homes with a basement or crawlspace typically route supply lines through accessible spaces rather than burying them in concrete, which makes a leak in those homes easier to spot early and far less expensive to reach when it happens.

A slab-on-grade home, common in newer single-story construction, doesn't have that same accessible space, which is exactly why the warm-floor symptom becomes such an important early warning sign rather than just an oddity to shrug off.

If you're building or renovating and have a choice in the matter, routing supply lines through walls or an accessible chase rather than under the slab avoids this entire category of problem for the life of the home. That's not a retrofit option for an existing slab home, but it's worth knowing if you're ever in a position to influence how a new build or major renovation routes its plumbing.

Local Note

Hiring For A Warm-Floor Leak In Kent

A warm spot can mean a hot water line leaking under the slab, and that repair is plumbing work. Washington L&I says plumbing contract work must be performed by a Licensed Plumbing Contractor under chapter 18.106 RCW, and that contractor must carry a $6,000 surety bond and general liability insurance.

Verify any contractor's registration with L&I before work starts. We serve Kent and nearby Covington and SeaTac.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is a warm floor patch always a slab leak?

It's the most common cause by far when the warmth is isolated to one specific area and persistent. Radiant floor heating, if your home has it, is the other possibility, and it's worth confirming which situation you're in before assuming the worst.

How urgent is a suspected slab leak?

It's worth addressing promptly rather than waiting, since the longer it runs, the more it can affect the foundation area and the more water gets wasted in the meantime.

Will fixing a slab leak require breaking up my whole floor?

No. Acoustic and thermal detection narrow the location closely enough that the repair opening is typically small and targeted, not a large section of the slab.

Can a slab leak happen with a cold water line too, not just hot?

Yes, though it's less commonly noticed since a cold line leak doesn't produce the same warm-floor signature. It's more often caught through a rising water bill or the sound of running water instead.

Can a slab leak affect more than one room?

Yes, if the affected line runs beneath multiple areas of the home, or if water has traveled some distance under the slab before finding its way up through the concrete.

Does a slab leak always show up as a warm spot?

Not always. It depends on how the specific line is routed and how deep it actually sits beneath the slab, which is why a rising water bill or the sound of running water are also genuinely worth watching for.

Can anyone repair a slab leak in Kent?

Not for pay. Washington requires plumbing contract work to be done by a Licensed Plumbing Contractor under chapter 18.106 RCW, with a bond and liability insurance. Public Health, Seattle and King County says an owner may do their own plumbing work, but a slab leak is rarely a homeowner repair.
