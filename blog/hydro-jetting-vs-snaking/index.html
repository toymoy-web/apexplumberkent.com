Hydro Jetting vs. Snaking: Which Does Your Drain Actually Need?

Both clear a clog. Only one actually cleans the pipe. Here's how to tell which one your drain needs.

Written by the Kent Apex Plumbing team Published September 2026

If you've had a drain snaked more than once for the same clog, you've already run into the actual limitation of cabling: it clears a path through whatever's blocking the pipe, but it doesn't clean the pipe itself. Hydro jetting does something genuinely different, and understanding the difference is the key to not paying for the same service call three times in a year.

What A Cable Snake Actually Does

A cable auger, the standard drain snake, works by punching or grinding a hole through a clog. It's fast, it's effective for a first-time clog, and it's genuinely the right tool for a lot of calls. What it doesn't do is scrape or clean the rest of the pipe wall. Grease coating, mineral scale, and root hairs lining the pipe stay right where they are. If those are the actual underlying cause, the clog comes back, often in the same spot, within a few months.

What Hydro Jetting Does Differently

Hydro jetting feeds a hose with a rotating nozzle through the line and uses high-pressure water to scour the full diameter of the pipe clean, not just punch through the blockage. For a line with grease buildup or root intrusion, this is the difference between a temporary fix and an actual clean pipe. It's especially relevant in neighborhoods with mature street trees near older clay or cast iron sewer laterals, where root hairs regularly find their way into pipe joints looking for moisture.

This comes up often enough on calls from Maple Valley, where larger lots and longer drain runs mean more pipe for buildup to accumulate in. We treat a repeat clog on a longer run as a strong candidate for jetting rather than another round of cabling.

How To Tell Which One You Actually Need

First-Time Clog, Single Fixture

A standard cable snake is usually the right, less expensive first step.

Same Spot Clogs Again Within Months

That pattern means the cause wasn't actually cleared, a strong sign jetting is worth it.

Multiple Drains Slow At Once

Often means buildup somewhere in the main line, which jetting clears more completely than cabling.

Camera Shows Grease Or Root Coating

A visual confirmation that the pipe wall itself needs cleaning, not just the blockage removed.

The Honest Trade-Off

Jetting costs more than a standard cable service, and it's genuinely not necessary for every clog. A first-time kitchen sink clog usually clears with standard drain cleaning and doesn't need the added step. But if you're on your second or third visit for the same drain, spending less on another cable service that doesn't hold isn't actually the cheaper option. A camera inspection before jetting confirms the pipe is in good enough structural condition for high pressure, since jetting isn't the right call on a line that's already cracked or badly deteriorated.

Cabling vs. Jetting, Side By Side

The table oversimplifies one thing worth calling out directly: jetting done on a structurally sound pipe is not riskier than cabling, it's simply a more thorough process that costs more because it accomplishes more. The added cost buys a genuinely cleaner pipe, not just a temporary opening.

What To Expect During A Jetting Service

A typical jetting visit starts with locating the nearest cleanout access, either inside near a main line or an exterior access point in the yard. The technician feeds the jetting hose through the line while monitoring water pressure, working the nozzle back and forth to scour the pipe wall along its full length rather than just pushing straight through. For a longer main line, this can take anywhere from 30 minutes to over an hour, depending on the line's length and how much buildup has accumulated.

Afterward, a lot of technicians will run the camera through the line again to confirm the pipe is actually clean, not just clear. That follow-up check matters, since the whole point of jetting over cabling is a genuinely clean pipe wall, and skipping the verification step defeats some of the purpose of choosing the more thorough method in the first place.

How Often Should A Recurring Root Problem Be Jetted?

If mature trees near your sewer lateral are the real cause of repeated root intrusion, a single jetting visit clears what's there today, but it doesn't stop new roots from growing back toward the pipe over time, especially if the tree itself isn't going anywhere. For a line with a known, ongoing root problem, an annual or every-other-year maintenance jetting is often more cost-effective than waiting for a full backup and paying for an emergency call.

A root barrier installed along the line's path is another option worth discussing if the root intrusion is severe enough to need jetting more than once a year. It's an added upfront cost, but it can meaningfully reduce how often the underlying problem returns, which matters if you're dealing with a genuinely mature, aggressive root system near an older clay or cast iron lateral.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

For clay, cast iron, and PVC lines in sound structural condition, yes. A camera inspection first confirms the pipe can handle it.

How much more does jetting cost than snaking?

It costs more than a standard cable service, though for a recurring clog it's often less expensive over time than repeated cabling that doesn't actually solve the problem.

Can I rent jetting equipment and do this myself?

We'd recommend against it on a pipe of unknown condition. Consumer-grade equipment used on a line with an existing crack can make the problem significantly worse.

Same Drain Clogging Again?

A camera inspection tells us if jetting is the right fix.
