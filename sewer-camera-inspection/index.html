Overview

Seeing What's Actually Wrong

A sewer camera inspection feeds a waterproof camera through the line to show exactly what's causing a backup, slow drain, or recurring clog. It's the tool that turns a guess into a confirmed diagnosis, whether the cause is root intrusion, a cracked joint, grease buildup, or a genuine structural collapse.

This matters most before a bigger job. A camera inspection can be the difference between paying for a repipe you didn't need and confirming that jetting alone will solve the problem. It can also catch a structural break early before it turns into a full backup in the house.

Kent Context

Camera Work Around Kent

A camera inspection is the standard first step for Kent homes on valley-floor laterals and for anyone buying near downtown, in Mill Creek, or on West Hill. The footage shows roots, offsets, and cracks before they become backups, and gives you something concrete to base a repair decision on.

Signs You Need This

When A Camera Inspection Makes Sense

A Clog Keeps Coming Back

If the same drain has been cleared more than once, a camera shows whether roots or a structural issue are the real cause.

Buying A Home With An Older Sewer Line

A pre-purchase camera inspection can catch a costly sewer problem before it becomes your problem.

Slow Drains Throughout The House

A camera confirms whether the main line has a partial blockage rather than an issue with individual fixtures.

Before A Major Repair Or Repipe

Confirming the exact location and cause first keeps a bigger job properly scoped instead of guessed at.

How We Fix It

How The Inspection Works

We feed a flexible camera through an existing cleanout or access point, recording footage as it travels the length of the line. A locator on the camera head lets us mark the exact depth and position of anything we find, which matters quite a bit if a repair ends up being needed later.

You get a straight explanation of what the footage shows, in plain, everyday terms, along with a copy of the recording. If everything looks clean, we'll tell you that too rather than manufacturing a problem to justify the visit. Any repair recommendation that follows meets the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Washington has adopted statewide.

This kind of documentation is also genuinely useful well beyond the immediate diagnosis. A recorded inspection can support an insurance claim if damage is later found, and it gives a homeowner a real record of the line's condition at a point in time, useful when comparing against a future inspection down the road.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I get a copy of the footage?

Yes, we provide a copy of the recording along with a plain-language explanation of what it shows.

Can a camera inspection find a leak too?

It's built to inspect drain and sewer lines specifically. Supply line leaks are found with acoustic and thermal detection instead.

Should I get one before buying a house?

It's a reasonable step for any home with an older sewer line, especially one with mature trees nearby, since root intrusion isn't visible from the surface.

How long does an inspection take?

Most single-line inspections are completed in under an hour.

Can a camera inspection be done on a very old clay pipe?

Yes, the camera works regardless of pipe material, and older clay lines are actually where this inspection tends to matter most given how commonly root intrusion affects them over time.
