Overview

Filtration For What You're Actually Noticing

Municipal water in Kent already meets safety standards, but taste, chlorine smell, and occasional sediment are still real, common complaints. A whole-home filtration system treats water at the point it enters the house, while an under-sink system targets just the drinking and cooking water at a single fixture.

The right choice depends entirely on what you're actually trying to solve and how your household uses water day to day. A chlorine taste complaint doesn't need the same system as a household dealing with sediment from an older service line, and we'll ask the right questions before recommending either one.

Signs You Need This

Common Reasons To Consider Filtration

Chlorine Taste Or Smell

A noticeable chlorine taste is common in municipal water and is one of the easiest issues to address with basic filtration.

Sediment Or Cloudiness

Visible particles or cloudy water can come from an older service line and may call for a sediment filter.

Wanting Better-Tasting Drinking Water

An under-sink system is a targeted, lower-cost option if drinking water taste is your only real concern.

Private Well Water Concerns

Well water isn't covered by municipal treatment at all, and filtration needs there depend entirely on your specific water test results.

Safety First

Before We Arrive

Know Your Main Shutoff

A whole-home system ties into your main line, so knowing where that shutoff is helps if anything needs adjusting after install.

Clear Under-Sink Access

For an under-sink system, clear out cleaning supplies and stored items so we have room to work.

Note Any Existing Water Concerns

Mention any past water quality test results or specific concerns so the right system gets recommended the first time.

Locate Your Water Heater If Whole-Home

A whole-home system's placement sometimes depends on where it sits relative to the water heater. Knowing that location speeds up the assessment.

How We Fix It

Choosing And Installing The Right System

For a taste or odor complaint, a simpler carbon-based filtration system, either whole-home or under-sink, usually resolves it completely. For sediment concerns, we'll check whether the issue is coming from the municipal supply or your home's own internal plumbing before recommending a fix.

Installation includes properly integrating the system with your existing plumbing and setting up a realistic filter replacement schedule so it keeps performing the way it did on day one.

Reverse osmosis systems go a step further than basic carbon filtration, removing a genuinely broader range of dissolved substances at the cost of a slower flow rate and more frequent filter changes. Most Kent households don't need that level of filtration for municipal water, and we'll say so rather than recommending the most expensive option by default.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need a whole-home system or just under-sink?

It depends on what you're trying to fix. If it's just drinking water taste, under-sink is usually enough and costs less. Whole-home makes more sense for sediment or a broader household concern.

Is Kent's water safe to drink without filtration?

Yes, municipal water here meets required safety standards. Filtration is about taste, smell, and sediment preference, not a safety necessity.

How often do filters need to be replaced?

It varies by system, but we'll set up a realistic replacement schedule as part of the installation so performance doesn't quietly decline over time.

Can you test my well water before recommending a system?

We can help arrange water testing so any filtration recommendation for a private well is based on your actual results, not a guess.

Does filtration reduce water pressure?

A properly sized system shouldn't cause a noticeable drop in pressure. An undersized filter for your household's water use is the most common cause when pressure does suffer.

How long does installation take?

An under-sink system is typically installed in under an hour, while a whole-home system takes longer depending on where it ties into your main line.
